# Talent & Leadership Storytelling Generator

## Purpose

Turn real work, lessons, and outcomes into clear stories that explain why the work matters without sounding promotional, generic, or self-congratulatory.

## Trigger When

Use this skill when the user asks to:

- Turn a project, event, initiative, milestone, or lesson into a story
- Create thought leadership or leadership messaging
- Build a presentation narrative or executive storyline
- Write a LinkedIn post, newsletter article, town hall message, or success story
- Explain the human and business value of HR, talent, Copilot, automation, learning, or change work
- Create an employee, team, or frontline success spotlight

## Inputs

Use available:

- What changed
- Why the work was needed
- Who the audience or beneficiaries were
- What actions were taken
- Verified outcomes or evidence
- Challenges, lessons, and next steps
- Desired channel and audience

If a result is not quantified, do not invent a metric. A credible qualitative outcome is better than a fabricated number.

## Narrative Principles

- Start with a meaningful tension, observation, or change
- Make the business and human relevance clear
- Use concrete details, not abstract HR language
- Center the work and lesson, not personal praise
- Show progress honestly, including what remains open
- End with a useful takeaway, action, or forward-looking point

## Story Framework Selection

Choose the framework that best fits the evidence.

### Challenge → Shift → Impact → Next

Best for transformations, process improvements, automation, and capability building.

### Observation → Lesson → Application

Best for leadership reflections and thought leadership.

### Before → Change → After

Best when the source clearly supports a previous and current state.

### Person/Team → Contribution → Broader Meaning

Best for recognition and employee spotlights. Focus on documented actions and impact, not personality judgments.

### Event → Insight → Commitment

Best for conference, workshop, or learning recaps.

### Problem → Experiment → Learning

Best when outcomes are still developing and the key value is what was learned.

## Workflow

1. Identify audience, channel, and intended outcome.
2. Review the source material.
3. Extract:
   - The central tension or need
   - The action taken
   - Verified evidence of impact
   - The most useful lesson
   - The next step
4. Select one narrative framework.
5. Write a one-sentence story thesis.
6. Build the narrative around that thesis.
7. Remove unsupported claims, hype, and generic praise.
8. Adapt length and structure for the channel.

## Channel Outputs

### Executive Presentation Narrative

Provide:

- Core message: One sentence
- Why now: Business context
- Story arc: 3 to 5 beats
- Proof points: Verified examples or metrics
- Leadership takeaway: What leaders should understand or do
- Closing line: Concise and memorable

### LinkedIn Post

Provide:

- A strong first two lines
- Short paragraphs with intentional whitespace
- One clear lesson
- A grounded closing thought or discussion question
- Three to five relevant hashtags only when useful

Do not use engagement bait, fake vulnerability, or invented statistics.

### Newsletter or Intranet Story

Provide:

- Headline
- Short summary
- Narrative body
- What this means for employees or leaders
- Next step or resource

### Town Hall Talking Points

Provide:

- Opening message
- Three key points
- One proof point for each
- Recognition where supported
- Clear close and action

### Success Story

Provide:

- Challenge
- Approach
- Outcome
- What made the difference
- What comes next

### Employee or Team Spotlight

Provide:

- Context
- Documented contribution
- Measurable or observable impact
- Connection to organizational values or priorities, only when supported

## Tone Modes

Select or infer one:

- Executive: Strategic, concise, business-linked
- Reflective: Personal but restrained and credible
- Educational: Practical and insight-led
- Celebratory: Appreciative without exaggeration
- Recruiting/Employer Brand: Candidate-relevant and authentic
- Change Story: Clear on the why, progress, and next step

## Source and Accuracy Guardrails

- Never invent metrics, quotes, testimonials, outcomes, dates, or participation.
- Clearly label planned, early, anecdotal, and measured outcomes.
- Do not claim causation when the source only shows correlation or sequence.
- Do not infer an employee's attitude, potential, motivation, personality, or performance.
- For employee stories, focus on documented work and impact.
- Do not disclose confidential HR, candidate, compensation, health, performance, or personal data.
- Obtain or note the need for appropriate approval before publishing identifiable employee stories externally.
- Keep company and product claims aligned with verified source material.

## Anti-Generic Rewrite Test

Replace or remove phrases like:

- “Our people are our greatest asset”
- “In today’s rapidly changing world”
- “Game-changing”
- “Revolutionary”
- “We are thrilled to announce”
- “A testament to”

Prefer a specific action, example, or lesson instead.

## Quality Check

Before returning the story, verify:

- The central point is clear
- The story is supported by actual evidence
- The business and people relevance are both visible
- The format fits the requested channel
- The tone is credible, not self-congratulatory
- No sensitive information or personal evaluation is included
- The ending gives the audience a useful takeaway or next step
